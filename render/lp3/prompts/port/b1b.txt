STYLE — identical in every scene of this film. Soft matte 3D animation in the style of a modern animated feature. Every surface is flat and chalky with no gloss, no specular highlights, no reflections and no metallic sheen. Gentle even daylight, soft contact shadows. Simplified friendly geometry — clean shapes, no clutter of tiny detail, no photorealism.

ONE SINGLE IMAGE — one continuous photograph filling the whole frame. Never a split screen, never a diptych, never stacked panels, never a comic strip, never a grid, never a border or inset.

CAMERA — this film's defining rule. The camera is locked on a tripod and square-on to its subject: level, frontal, facing straight ahead, horizon dead flat and horizontal, at the eye height of a standing adult. The composition is calm and centred. The same camera height in every scene of the film. No low hero angle, no high or bird's-eye angle, no tilt.

LIGHT — bright clean daylight, soft and even. Everything is fresh, clean and in good order: no night, no dusk, no rain, no dust, no litter, no rust, no broken thing, no clutter. The mood is upbeat and easy.

COLOUR LAW — obeyed absolutely. The ONLY saturated colour anywhere in the frame is the green of the little green car. Everything else in the world is strictly desaturated: deep navy, dark slate grey, warm off-white paper, and medium neutral grey. No blue sky, no orange sunset, no red, no yellow, no amber indicator lamps, no colourful signage, no colourful clothing, no coloured lights. Other vehicles are grey. Buildings are grey and off-white. The sky is a pale neutral off-white haze. Nothing competes with the green car.

PLACE — recognisably Egyptian. Cairo and its newer suburbs: low concrete apartment blocks with balconies, palm trees, clean wide boulevards, desert-edge ring roads.

STRICTLY FORBIDDEN — no readable text anywhere in the image: no letters, no words, no numbers, no digits, no signage lettering, no logos, no badges, no watermarks. Every sign, every board and every panel is completely blank.

THE CHARACTER — the small green car in the reference image, exactly as it appears there. An ordinary everyday Egyptian FOUR-DOOR SALOON: three-box, a clear separate boot at the back, a long flat bonnet, four side windows, a level roofline, soft rounded friendly forms. Never a short, tall, round two-door bubble hatchback; it always has a boot. It is a FULL-SIZE ROAD CAR, the same size as the cars around it — never a toy, never a miniature. Its paint is flat matte vivid spring-green, one uniform saturated colour, chalky and non-glossy — never pale pastel mint. Plain grey five-spoke steel wheels.

ITS FACE — large round expressive eyes set INSIDE THE WINDSHIELD GLASS, warm dark brown irises, bright and cheerful. Its mouth is its GRILLE: a large recessed opening low in the front, a wide crescent whose ends sweep upward so the opening itself reads as a warm closed-mouth smile. Directly below the grille sits a blank number-plate recess, so the nose is fully occupied: there is NO smooth bumper below the grille and NO second smiling crease pressed into the paint. The headlamps are plain lamps and are never eyes.

ITS STRIPES — two narrow flat white bands run dead centre along the top of the car, straddling its exact centreline, straight from bonnet to roof to boot lid, parallel and of equal constant width. Never angled, never swept, never offset.

ITS MARKINGS — the wordmark "140 Auto" in warm off-white, both words together on ONE line, in plain flat lettering, and TINY: no taller than a door handle, a small fraction of the panel it sits on. On the bonnet and low on each front door. Never large, never bold, never stacked, never the number alone, never in a circle or roundel, never a race number or emblem. Number-plate recesses are empty. No other text, numerals, decals or stickers on the car.

WHERE ITS FACE IS — the face exists ONLY on the front. The eyes are in the FRONT windshield and nowhere else. The rear windscreen, the boot lid, the rear bumper and every side window are plain ordinary glass and bodywork with NO eyes, NO mouth and NO face. With the camera behind the car we see only its plain back, with no face visible at all.

ONLY THIS CAR IS ALIVE. No other vehicle has eyes, a face, a mouth or any expression. Every other car's grille is a PLAIN STRAIGHT FLAT RECTANGLE of slats with square corners — it never curves, arches or reads as a smile, and no other car has any grin or smiling crease anywhere on its bumper or bodywork. The crescent smiling grille belongs to the little green car alone. Every other car is an ordinary lifeless saloon of the SAME SIZE as it, never larger, never towering over it.

SCENE — a bright, clean, orderly row of small Egyptian car showrooms along a wide suburban boulevard, seen dead-on from across the road. Each showroom is a simple pale concrete unit with a wide plate-glass front, and one grey car stands on a low pale plinth in front of each. The units recede evenly to left and right in a straight line parallel to the camera, like a row of identical pale boxes. Above each unit is a completely blank rectangular sign panel with no lettering of any kind. A few palm trees stand between the units. The forecourt tarmac is clean and empty. Every one of the parked cars is an ordinary lifeless grey vehicle.

The camera is level and square-on to the row; the row runs straight across the frame, horizontal.

The little green car has stopped at the RIGHT of centre, in profile, drawn level with one particular car on its plinth. It stays fully inside the frame at the same natural distance and the same size as before. That one car's PLINTH is now lit with a clean pool of soft green light: the top surface and the front face of the plinth glow soft green, and a soft green pool of light spills onto the pale tarmac around its base, so that one bay stands out clearly from all the others. THE PARKED CAR STANDING ON THAT PLINTH DOES NOT CHANGE COLOUR AT ALL — its paint stays exactly the same dull neutral grey as every other parked car in the row. It does not turn green, it is not tinted green, and no green appears on its bodywork. THERE IS STILL EXACTLY ONE GREEN CAR IN THIS PICTURE: the little green car with the face. Every other car in the frame is grey.
