STYLE — identical in every scene of this film. Soft matte 3D animation in the style of a modern animated feature. Every surface is flat and chalky with no gloss, no specular highlights, no reflections and no metallic sheen. Gentle even daylight, soft contact shadows. Simplified friendly geometry — clean shapes, no clutter of tiny detail, no photorealism.

ONE SINGLE IMAGE — one continuous photograph filling the whole frame. Never a split screen, never a diptych, never stacked panels, never a comic strip, never a grid, never a border or inset.

CAMERA — this film's defining rule. The camera is locked on a tripod and square-on to its subject: level, frontal, facing straight ahead, horizon dead flat and horizontal, at the eye height of a standing adult. The composition is calm and centred. The same camera height in every scene of the film. No low hero angle, no high or bird's-eye angle, no tilt.

LIGHT — bright clean daylight, soft and even. Everything is fresh, clean and in good order: no night, no dusk, no rain, no dust, no litter, no rust, no broken thing, no clutter. The mood is upbeat and easy.

COLOUR LAW — obeyed absolutely. The ONLY saturated colour anywhere in the frame is the green of the little green car. Everything else in the world is strictly desaturated: deep navy, dark slate grey, warm off-white paper, and medium neutral grey. No blue sky, no orange sunset, no red, no yellow, no amber indicator lamps, no colourful signage, no colourful clothing, no coloured lights. Other vehicles are grey. Buildings are grey and off-white. The sky is a pale neutral off-white haze. Nothing competes with the green car.

PLACE — recognisably Egyptian. Cairo and its newer suburbs: low concrete apartment blocks with balconies, palm trees, clean wide boulevards, desert-edge ring roads.

STRICTLY FORBIDDEN — no text, no letters, no words, no numbers, no digits, no signage lettering, no logos, no badges, no watermarks anywhere in the image. Every sign and panel is completely blank.

THE CHARACTER — the small green car shown in the reference images, exactly as it appears there and nowhere altered. An ordinary everyday Egyptian FOUR-DOOR SALOON: a three-box car with a clear separate boot at the back, a long flat bonnet at the front, four side windows and a level roofline, with soft rounded friendly forms. It is never a short, tall, round two-door bubble hatchback, and it always has a boot. It is a FULL-SIZE ROAD CAR, the same size as the other cars around it — never a toy, never a miniature, never a tiny round two-door bubble car. Its paint is flat matte vivid spring-green, one uniform saturated colour, chalky and completely non-glossy — never pale pastel mint. Plain grey five-spoke steel wheels.

ITS FACE — large, round, expressive eyes set INSIDE THE WINDSHIELD GLASS, warm dark brown irises, bright and cheerful. Its mouth is its GRILLE: a large recessed opening low in the front, shaped as a wide crescent whose ends sweep upward so the opening itself reads as a warm closed-mouth smile. Directly below the grille sits a blank number-plate recess, so the nose is fully occupied and there is no smooth bumper below the grille and no second smiling crease pressed into the paint. The headlamps are ordinary plain lamps and are never eyes.

ITS STRIPES — two narrow flat white bands run dead centre along the top of the car, straddling its exact centreline, straight from bonnet to roof to boot lid, parallel and of equal constant width. Never angled, never swept, never offset to one side.

ITS MARKINGS — the wordmark "140 Auto" in warm off-white, small and quiet as an ordinary model badge, on the bonnet and low on each front door. It is always the two words TOGETHER on ONE SINGLE LINE in plain flat lettering, and it is always TINY — no taller than the door handle beside it, occupying a small fraction of the panel it sits on. Never large, never bold, never stacked on two lines, never filling or spanning a door or a bonnet. Never the number on its own, never inside a circle, never a roundel, never a race number, never a badge or emblem. Its number-plate recesses are empty. No other text, numerals, decals or stickers anywhere on the car.

WHERE ITS FACE IS — the face exists ONLY on the front of the car. The eyes are in the FRONT windshield and nowhere else. The rear windscreen, the boot lid, the rear bumper and every side window are plain ordinary glass and plain ordinary bodywork with NO eyes, NO mouth and NO face on them. When the camera is behind the car we therefore see only its plain back, with no face visible at all. Never duplicate the face onto the back or the sides.

ONLY THIS CAR IS ALIVE. No other vehicle anywhere in the scene has eyes, a face or any expression. Every other car is an ordinary lifeless vehicle with plain blank headlights and no character whatsoever. Do not give eyes to any other car. Do not give a mouth to any other car either: every other car's grille is a PLAIN STRAIGHT HORIZONTAL RECTANGLE of ordinary slats, level and flat across the whole of its width, with square corners. No other car's grille curves, arches, sweeps upward at the ends or reads as a smile, and no other car has any grin, crease, curve or smiling line anywhere on its bumper or bodywork. The crescent smiling grille belongs to the little green car alone. Every other car is also the SAME SIZE as the little green car — an ordinary saloon of ordinary height, never larger, never towering over it.

SCENE — a clean, bright, enclosed inspection bay, composed for a TALL vertical frame: plain pale concrete walls and a plain pale ceiling, open toward the camera, lit evenly and brightly from above. A shallow pale rectangular inspection platform is set into the pale concrete floor. There is no street, no traffic and no distant skyline — the bay's own back wall closes the frame behind. Everything is clean, bright, empty and in good order.

ONE ORDINARY GREY SALOON CAR stands on the platform in the centre of the bay, perfectly square-on to the camera and facing straight down the lens. WE SEE IT FROM THE FRONT: its bonnet, its grille and both its headlamps face us squarely and its two front wheels are level with each other. It is an ordinary lifeless vehicle: blank plain headlights, a plain straight flat rectangular grille, no eyes, no mouth, no smile, no grin, no curve and no expression of any kind. It sits in the lower half of the tall frame, with the bay's pale wall and ceiling filling the space above it.

ONE PERSON is in the bay: a calm Egyptian inspection engineer, an adult standing on the floor beside the grey car's front wing, turned toward the car and looking down at it, holding a flat tablet. He is FULLY CLOTHED from neck to ankle in loose, plain, undecorated dark navy long-sleeved work overalls and plain work boots — no bright colour, no logo, no lettering, no badge, no high-visibility vest, no hard hat. He is seen at a normal working distance, small and complete within the frame, standing upright and entirely inside the picture from his head to his feet; he is never close to the camera, never cropped and never large in the frame. He is an ordinary calm professional at work, unhurried and pleasant, doing nothing but looking at the car.

The camera is level and square-on to the bay.

THE LITTLE GREEN CAR IS SMALL IN THE FRAME AND COMPLETELY INSIDE IT. It stands at the side of the bay, well back and at a distance from the camera, whole and entire, with clear pale floor between it and the edge of the picture, seen in three-quarter view and nosed in toward the grey car, its headlamps casting a soft pool of light across the grey car's near flank. It is never cropped and never touching the edge of the frame.
