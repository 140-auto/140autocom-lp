SCENE — a clean, bright, enclosed inspection bay: a simple room-like service bay with plain pale concrete walls on three sides and a plain pale ceiling, open toward the camera, lit evenly and brightly from above. A shallow pale rectangular inspection platform is set into the pale concrete floor. There is no street, no traffic, no flyover and no distant skyline — the bay's own back wall closes the frame behind. Everything is clean, bright, empty and in good order.

ONE ORDINARY GREY SALOON CAR stands on the platform in the exact centre of the bay, perfectly square-on to the camera and facing straight down the lens. WE SEE IT FROM THE FRONT: its bonnet, its grille and both its headlamps face us squarely and its two front wheels are level with each other. We do not see its side. It is an ordinary lifeless vehicle: blank plain headlights, a plain straight flat rectangular grille, no eyes, no mouth, no smile, no grin, no curve and no expression of any kind. It is an ordinary modest saloon the same size and height as the little green car — not a large car, not a tall car, not towering.

ONE PERSON is in the bay: a calm, friendly Egyptian inspection engineer, an adult standing on the floor close beside the grey car's FRONT LEFT WING, turned toward the car and looking attentively down at it, holding a flat tablet in one hand. He wears plain, undecorated dark navy work overalls and nothing else of note — no bright colour anywhere on him, no logo, no lettering, no badge, no high-visibility vest, no hard hat. He is an ordinary calm professional at work, unhurried and pleasant, not alarmed and not pointing at anything. He is the only person anywhere in the frame. He stands in EXACTLY the same place, in the same posture, in both frames, and does not move at all.

The camera is level, square-on to the bay and STANDING WELL BACK, so that the whole width of the bay, both cars and the engineer all sit comfortably inside the picture with clear empty floor visible at the left edge and at the right edge. The grey car stays dead centre and the engineer stays in exactly the same place in the frame in both frames.

THE LITTLE GREEN CAR IS SMALL IN THE FRAME AND COMPLETELY INSIDE IT. It stands well back at the side of the bay, at a distance from the camera, whole and entire, with a clear margin of empty pale floor between it and the edge of the picture. It takes up no more than about one fifth of the width of the picture. It is never close to the camera, never large in the frame, never cropped, never cut off by the edge and never touching the edge. Its whole body, from its front bumper to its boot lid, is visible.

The bonnet of the grey car is CLOSED. The little green car is at the far LEFT of the bay, seen in three-quarter view, nosed in toward the grey car, its headlamps casting a soft pool of light across the grey car's near flank.
