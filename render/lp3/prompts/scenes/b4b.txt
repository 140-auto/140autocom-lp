SCENE — a wide clean stretch of pale tarmac on the edge of a Cairo suburb, seen dead-on. Far back and small, a low line of pale apartment blocks and a few palm trees. Bright even daylight, pale off-white hazy sky, no blue. Nothing else is in frame: no other vehicles, no people, no buildings nearby, no signage.

THE SUBJECT OF THIS PICTURE IS A STACK OF PAPER. Standing on the tarmac in the RIGHT half of the frame is one tidy tower of loose paper sheets, resting squarely on the ground, edges aligned, upright, never fanned, never scattered.

THE PAPERS ARE NOT BLANK. Every sheet is covered in dense handwritten cursive scribble — looped flowing pen strokes in neat straight lines, a few ruled boxes, a signature-like flourish near the bottom. It is pure squiggle and spells nothing: no real letters of any alphabet, no numerals, nothing anyone could read. From a distance each page plainly has writing on it and has plainly been filled in. Fine dark grey-blue ink on warm off-white paper. A blank white page is WRONG.

The little green car is parked in the LEFT half of the frame, square-on to the camera, facing straight down the lens, both eyes visible, calm and pleased. It is at a comfortable middle distance, completely inside the frame and uncropped. The camera is level, square-on and does not move between the two frames; the car stays in exactly the same place at exactly the same size in both.

THIS IS ONE SINGLE PHOTOGRAPH of one single scene, filling the whole frame. It is NOT a two-panel sheet, NOT a diptych, NOT two views of the car side by side, NOT a character study. There is exactly ONE green car in the picture.

The stack has GROWN into a tall neat tower of sheets standing about as high as the car's roof. Same single stack, same spot, just far taller. The green car has not moved and nothing else has changed.
