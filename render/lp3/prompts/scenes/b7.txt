SCENE — the closing frame, and the fullest, warmest, happiest image in the film. A wide clean suburban avenue seen dead-on, in warm soft late-afternoon light — still bright and clear, never dusk, never orange. Neat rows of ordinary grey cars are parked along both sides of the avenue, receding evenly into the distance on the left and on the right. Behind them, low pale concrete apartment blocks with balconies, and tall palm trees spaced along the kerbs. The paving and the tarmac are clean and freshly swept. The frame is full and lively and well-populated with all of this, but calm and orderly, with nothing broken and nothing cluttered. Plenty of pale open sky across the top of the frame. No people. Every parked car is an ordinary lifeless grey vehicle with no face.

The camera is level and square-on to the avenue, which runs straight away from the camera down the centre of the frame.

The little green car is parked in the middle distance, exactly on the centre line of the avenue, square-on to the camera and facing straight down the lens, both eyes clearly visible and looking directly forward, bright and cheerful and pleased with itself. It is comfortably clear of every other car, with clean open ground around it.
